ABSTRACT
With the rapid development of information technology, there is a growing demand for people in
indoor location services.This paper proposes a novel high-precision indoor three-dimensional
positioning algorithm for single light emitting diode (LED). By using three tilted photodetectors
(PD)and one horizontal PD as receivers.Calculating the ratio of received optical power of the
three tilted PDs to the horizontal PD respectively. According to the relationship of position be-
tween the PDs and the tilt angle, azimuth angle, placement angle of the tilted PD and other
parameters, establish a mathematical relationship between the received optical power ratio and
the receiver's three-dimensional coordinates, calculate the three-dimensional coordinates of the
receiver at any position. The simulation results show that in the positioning area of
1 × 1 × 1.5 m3, when the inclination angle α is 25°, the azimuth angle di ﬀerence θ is 90°, the
placement angle ω1=0°, ω2=90°, ω3=180°, the distance r between the horizontal PD center and
the tilted PD center is 1 cm, the positioning accuracy is better, the point with the positioning
error within 3 cm accounts for 84 % of the total test points, and the average positioning error is
2.52 cm. Compared with the traditional indoor positioning technology, the cost of the positioning
technology used in this paper is greatly reduced, the positioning accuracy is signi ﬁcantly im-
proved,and can get three-dimensional coordinates.
1. Introduction
Recently, Global Positioning System (GPS) has been widely used in car navigation, mapping and other ﬁelds.When it comes to
indoor positioning, the indoor environment is more complicated, the signal from the satellite is blocked by obstacles, the positioning
error by GPS in the room will become very big [ 1]. With the development of science and technology, researchers have proposed many
indoor positioning technologies based on wireless electromagnetic waves, such as infrared positioning, wireless local area network
(WLAN), radio frequency identi ﬁcation (RFID), Bluetooth, ultra-wideband (UWB), etc. [ 2]. However, these positioning technologies
both have obvious disadvantages: Although infrared positioning technology and UWB positioning technology have higher positioning
accuracy, but additional equipment is required, and the cost is high. The price of the Bluetooth device is relatively expensive, and for
the complex environment, the stability of the Bluetooth system is poor, and the interference by the noise signal is large. RFID
technology has a short working distance, and the positioning error is large, the average positioning error is as high as 1 m. The WIFI
transceiver can only cover an area within a radius of 90 m, and is easily interfered by other signals, the average positioning error is as
high as 2∼ 3m [3]. Using visible light communication (VLC) for indoor positioning is a new way to solve indoor positioning problems
[4]. The frequency range of visible light is much wider than that of RF, and there is no electromagnetic interference, which is suitable

for hospitals, mines and other places. The visibility of visible light relative to RF signals is better, more convenient and fast, and the
positioning accuracy is also high, which has become a hot spot of research on optical communication researchers at this stage [ 5].
Traditional indoor visible light localization methods are mainly based on Time of arrival (TOA), Time Di ﬀerence of arrival
(TDOA), Angle of arrival (AOA), received signal strength indication (RSSRI) method, sensor and imaging method [6 ]. Among them,
although the TOA method is simple in principle, it requires strict synchronization between the transmitting end and the receiving end
clock, and requires high hardware. Similarly, the TDOA and AOA methods have higher hardware requirements [ 7]. Image sensor
imaging involves image processing and has a long positioning time [ 8].
The visible light positioning based on LED lights can also be classi ﬁed according to the number of light sources, one is based on
multiple LED light sources for position determination, the other is to perform indoor positioning according to a single LED [ 9].
Nowadays, domestic and foreign research on multi-lamp positioning and single-lamp positioning has been done [ 10]. For multi-lamp
positioning technology. In 2016, Yin, F. X. et al. proposed an indoor visible light reverse positioning system. The experimental results
show that the positioning error of the system is less than 0.2 m [ 11]. In 2018, Li, N et al. proposed an indoor positioning system based
on visible light communication technology. The simulation results show that the centimeter-level positioning accuracy can be ob-
tained at 5 heights in the 5 × 5 × 4 m 3 space. [12]. In 2018, Wang, Q et al. proposed an indoor visible light localization system based
on gesture recognition, which achieved decimeter-level positioning accuracy. [ 13]. In 2019, Chuang, Y. et al. proposed a localization
algorithm combining repetition unit and machine learning: The second-order regression ML algorithm and the polynomial three-edge
ML algorithm were discussed respectively, and the centimeter-level positioning accuracy was obtained [ 14]. For single-lamp posi-
tioning technology, in 2017, Zhang, R et al. proposed a visible light positioning system based on a single LED lamp, which achieved
centimeter-level positioning accuracy in the range of 3 × 3m 2,and solved the problem of sparse layout of indoor LED lights and the
positioning accuracy caused by insu ﬃcient receiver ﬁeld of view. [ 15]. In 2018, Yu, X. H. et al. proposed a visible light positioning
system using a single LED lamp and a new type of receiver. The receiver consists of a horizontal photodetector (PD) and two tilted
PDs. The experimental results show that this system can avoid inter-cell interference caused by di ﬀerent light sources, and it ’s
suitable for scenes with limited number of LEDs [ 16]. In 2019, Ji, Y. Q. et al. proposed a single LED lamp indoor positioning system
based on CMOS image sensor. This system can achieve centimeter-level positioning accuracy regardless of the horizontal or oblique
position of the mobile phone [ 17]. In summary, the indoor positioning of a single LED lamp has the following advantages compared
to multi-lamp positioning:
1) Solving the problem that the positioning accuracy of some indoor LED lights is sparse and the accuracy of the receiver is in-
suﬃcient.
2) Solving the problem of large positioning error caused by inter-symbol interference between lamps and lamps in multi-lamp
positioning [ 18].
3) The positioning speed is fast and the algorithm is relatively simple.
The single LED lamp positioning algorithm proposed in this paper can not only achieve high-precision three-dimensional posi-
tioning, but also signi ﬁcantly improve the positioning accuracy and greatly reduce the equipment cost compared with the traditional
wireless electromagnetic wave indoor positioning technology. Compared with the traditional multi-lamp positioning technology, the
proposed algorithm can e ﬀectively solve the problem of large positioning error caused by the interference between symbols of
multiple light sources, and does not need to distinguish the optical signals emitted by multiple light sources, which reduces the
complexity of the equipment and makes the positioning time faster.

3. Simulation of positioning system
In order to evaluate the performance of the positioning system, an experimental system is constructed in this paper. As is shown in
Fig. 2, the length, width and height of the initial positioning area are set to 1 m, 1 m, and 1.5 m respectively, and the coordinates of
the LED light source are (0.5, 0.5, 1.5). The interval between adjacent test heights is 0.2 m. on each test height, 7 × 7 test points are
set. The initial value of the distance r between the horizontal PD center and the inclined PD center is set to 1 cm, the initial value of
the azimuth di ﬀerence θ of the adjacent two oblique PDs is set to 90°, the placement angles ω1, ω2, and ω3 of the three tilted PDs are
set to 0°, 90°, and 180°respectively. This paper discusses the factors about what a ﬀecting the positioning error: including the tilt angle
α of the tilt PD, the azimuth angle di ﬀerence θ of the adjacent two tilted PDs, the placement angle ωi of the three tilted PDs (i = 1, 2,
3), and the distance r between the center of horizontal PD tilted PD. So these inﬂ uencing factors will be discussed separately below:
Firstly, when θ、r、ωi remain unchanged, only the inclination angle α of inclined PD is changed. The simulation results show that
when the inclination angle is greater than 30 degrees and the receiver is measured at 1.2 m height, the receiver can not locate at some
edge test points. This is because when the inclined angle of inclined PD is greater than 30 degrees, the incident angle of inclined PD at
some test edge points is larger than the ﬁeld of view of inclined PD. According to formula (1), the optical power received by inclined
PD at some test edge points is zero. So in the course of positioning, the inclination angle of inclined PD should be less than 30 degrees.
In order to describe more clearly of the variation of positioning error with inclination angle a, Fig. 3 shows the distribution of
positioning error of receiver at 0.4 m altitude when inclination angle a varies at 10, 15, 20 and 25 degrees.
It can be seen from Fig. 3 that when the test height of the receiver is constant, as the tilt angle α increases, the positioning error of
the edge test point gradually decreases. Table 1 shows the positioning error when the tilt angle α is changed at 10°, 15°, 20°, 25°and
when the test height of the receiver is 0.4 m.
According to Table 1, when other factors remain unchanged and the receiver measures at the same altitude, the bigger inclination
angle of inclined PD is, the smaller average positioning error is.
When α is constant, the positioning errors of the receiver are also di ﬀerent at di ﬀerent test altitudes. Fig. 4 shows the distribution
of positioning errors of the receiver at 0.2 m, 0.4 m, 0.6 m and 0.8 m altitudes when α equals to15°.
It can be seen from Fig. 4, when the tilt angle of the tilted PD is constant, the positioning error of the edge test points increases
slightly with the increase of the test height of the receiver. The average positioning errors of the receiver at 0.2 m, 0.4 m, 0.6 m and
0.8 m altitudes are shown in Table 2 when a is 15°.
It can be seen from Table 2, when the tilt angle is constant, the average positioning error increases slightly with the increase of the
test height of the receiver. Analysis of Tables 1 and 2 leads to conclusions.：when other factors remain unchanged, the bigger the a is,
the smaller the average positioning error is. When the tilt angle alpha is ﬁxed, the average positioning error increases slightly with the
increase of the receiver ’s test height.

4. Conclusion
This paper proposes a novel indoor three-dimensional positioning algorithm for single LED lamps, using a single LED lamp as the
transmitter, three tilt PDs and one horizontal PD as the receiver to receive the light intensity signal, and each tilted PD receiving
optical power and horizontal PD receiving. Compared with the power light, the received optical power ratio is substituted into the
positioning algorithm, and the three-dimensional coordinates of the receiver are calculated to complete the three-dimensional po-
sitioning.
In this paper, the main factors a ﬀecting the positioning error are studied, including the tilt angle α of the tilt receiver, the azimuth
angle di ﬀerent θ of the tilt receiver, the distance r between the center of the horizontal PD and the tilt PD center, and the placing
angle ω of the tilt PD. The simulation results show that when the other inﬂ uencing factors are constant, when the tilt angle α is within
a certain range of variation, the positioning error decreases as the tilt angle α increases. When α is constant, the receiver is tested. As
the height increases, the positioning error increases slightly. When only changing the azimuth di ﬀerence θ of the tilt receiver, when
θ < 90°, the positioning error decreases gradually with the increase of θ. When 90° < θ < 135°, the positioning error tends to be
stable with the change of θ. When θ > 135°, the positioning error suddenly increases. When only the distance r between the hor-
izontal PD center and the inclined PD center is changed, the positioning error increases remarkably as r increases. After analysis, the

algorithm is simple and novel compared with the traditional indoor positioning algorithm. It can e ﬀectively solve the problem of poor
positioning accuracy caused by interference between multiple light source symbols in multi-light source positioning, and it does not
need to be more The optical signals emitted by the light sources are distinguished, which reduces the complexity of the device and the
