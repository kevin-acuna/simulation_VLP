Zhenyu Wang

Abstract— In recent years, single-LED-based visible light posi-
tioning (VLP) systems have been developed because of their
low complexity, simple configuration, and feasibility for indoor
application scenarios. In this article, first, we describe the
system model of the single-tilted-rotatable-photodetector (STRP)-
based receiver in single-LED scenario considering the first-order
reflection signal. Then, we derive the Cramer–Rao lower bound
(CRLB) of the positioning error for the received signal strength
(RSS)-based positioning system using the discussed model and
select proper inclination and rotation angles according to the
derived CRLB. In addition, we analyze the least-square (LS)
positioning method and propose an improved gray wolf optimiza-
tion (IGWO)-based localization algorithm with the assistance
of the LS method. Finally, the proposed LS-IGWO positioning
algorithm has been evaluated in terms of accuracy and latency via
software simulations and field tests. Simulation and experiment
results show that using the RSS-based positioning model, the
proposed LS-IGWO algorithm can achieve centimeter-level posi-
tioning accuracy and performs best compared with the original
LS, the original GWO, single IGWO, pure LS-GWO, and the
existing fingerprint method.
Index Terms— Cramer–Rao lower bound (CRLB), gray wolf
optimization (GWO), least square (LS), received signal strength
(RSS), single LED, visible light positioning (VLP).

[4], Bluetooth [5], radio frequency identification (RFID) [6],
ultrawideband (UWB) [7], and visible light communication
(VLC) [8], [9], have been well developed in recent years.
Among them, VLC-based IPSs have been paid much attention

VLC-based positioning systems usually use one or more
LED lamps as the transmitter to help the users get their
position. Most existing visible light positioning (VLP) sys-
tems use multiple LEDs to achieve high-accuracy positioning
because of its strong stability, wide lighting range, and simple
receiver structure. However, for a small room, using one LED
is sufficient to achieve lighting, so it is a more cost-efficient
solution to deploy single LED. On the one hand, for single-
LED-based VLP systems, compared with the multi-LEDs, the
hardware signal processing delay at the receiver is much lower,
because it does not need to use multiple access protocols to
distinguish signals from different LEDs, and that improves
the real-time positioning performance. In addition, single-LED
systems can avoid the design of LED’s layout, the interference
between LEDs, and the waste of light resources, making it
more convenient for the VLP implementation [10]. On the
other hand, the designs of high-precision positioning algorithm
and appropriate receiver are still challenging for single-LED
scenarios. Therefore, in practice, it is necessary to decide to
use one or multiple LEDs according to the actual size of
the measured room and performance requirements, and VLP
systems with single LED can be considered as a cost-efficient
IPS solution for a small room.
In VLP systems, received signal strength (RSS) detected by
photodetectors (PDs) is more widely used, because they do not
need additional synchronization maintenance or high-precision
image acquisition devices. Therefore, RSS-PD-based VLP
systems can be considered a low-cost solution to the indoor
positioning problem [11]. In [12], the impact of the multipath
reflection signal on positioning performance was analyzed
for RSS-PD-based indoor VLP systems. Moreover, in [13]
and [14], the performance limits of LED-based positioning
and orientation systems were studied in non-line-of-sight
(NLOS) environments. Fingerprint and least-square (LS)-
based VLP algorithms considering multipath reflection were
studied in [15] and [16], respectively. However, these studies
are all implemented in multi-LED scenarios. For single-LED
scenes, there are few related studies, so it is necessary to
analyze the performance of the VLP system in this case.

taken as the device for sensing optical signals [17]. In the

receiver carrying PD is equipped with a long rotation arm,

fingerprint-based positioning algorithm proposed in [10] has
slightly higher complexity. Small-size receivers are designed

single-LED single-PD (SLSP)-based IPS, and the performance
of fingerprint-based localization algorithms usually deterio-

above problems, we further analyze the STRP-based receiver

of the LS method.
3) The GWO algorithm is implemented in single-LED
scenario to estimate the position of the TN using the
fingerprint database generated by the STRP-based VLC
channel model.
4) We introduce three improvement mechanisms, namely,
nonlinear convergence factor, weighted update strat-
egy, and mutation of the head wolf into the proposed
LS-IGWO algorithm to address the local optimum prob-
lems.
5) We evaluate the performances of the original LS [21],
the original GWO [22], single IGWO, pure LS-GWO,
the fingerprint method [20], and the proposed LS-IGWO

hnlos(t)= hnlos1(t)+ hnlos2(t)+···+ hnlosl(t)+··· (3)
where hnlosl(t) denotes the lth-order reflection CIR.
The spherical LED-based VLC transmission system is usu-

LOS and the first-order reflection components account for
a large proportion in the receiver, while the second and

Fig. 7 shows the curves of the averaged CRLB and PD
incidence angle ψ when the PD inclination angle θ varies
from 5◦ to 35◦. The number of rotation angles N is 4, and
the averaged CRLB is obtained from 576 different positions
uniformly distributed in the room. It can be seen that as θ
increases, CRLB drops, and at the same time, the positioning
accuracy is also improved gradually. This is because when
the tilt angle θ increases, the difference between the received
power from each rotation angle becomes larger, and that
provides more quantity of information about online samples.
Therefore, the CRLB gradually declines with the increase of
FIM.
However, observing the blue curve in Fig. 7, the incidence
angle ψ also increases when θ becomes larger, so it is
necessary to set the value of ψ within the PD FoV angle ψc.
When the receiver is located at (4.8, 4.8, 1) and the rotation
angle is 45◦ (the receiver is at the farthest position and faces
away from the LED representing the worst situation in the
localization), ψ is within ψc as θ varies from 5◦ to 20◦, and
that will ensure the PD to precisely detect optical signals in
the whole positioning area.

need to consider the convenience of practical implementation.

180◦, and 270 ◦, and that means we only need to align the
rotation reference line with the coordinate axis. Considering
both positioning accuracy and practical implementation, we set
θ= 20◦ for N= 4.

VI. C ONCLUSION
In this article, we evaluated the theoretical performance of
an existing STRP-based receiver in the SLSP scenarios and
then proposed an LS-IGWO-based VLP algorithm to mitigate
the impact of the first-order reflection optical signals. Accord-
ing to the derived CRLB of the positioning error, the proper
angles of inclination and rotation are selected. Simulation and
field test results show that for different LED orientations,
the proposed LS-IGWO positioning algorithm has higher
positioning accuracy and stronger stability than the original
LS [21], the original GWO [22], single IGWO, pure LS-
GWO, and the existing fingerprint positioning algorithm [20].
Therefore, the proposed LS-IGWO positioning algorithm with
STRP is effective and feasible and can be considered as a
competitive solution for SLSP-based IPS.
In our future work, we will focus on developing RSS-based
VLP systems, including more accurate single-LED-based
positioning schemes, real-time positioning algorithms, simul-
taneous positioning and orientation for the receiver, LED
orientation estimation, and system implementation for 6G
applications.
