\title{3-D Visible Light Positioning With a Single LED and a Single Reorientable Photodiode: Bounds, Calibration, and Experimental Validation}
\author{Author names and affiliations to be confirmed}
\markboth{IEEE Transactions on Instrumentation and Measurement}{Single-LED, Single-PD 3-D Positioning With a Reorientable Receiver}
\maketitle
\begin{abstract}
Visible light positioning (VLP) with a single light-emitting diode (LED) is attractive where only one luminaire is in view, but a single received signal strength (RSS) measurement cannot determine a three-dimensional (\mbox{3-D}) position. Existing centimeter-level systems that reorient a single photodiode (PD) estimate a two-dimensional position at a known height and assume a cosine response. This paper estimates the \mbox{3-D} position of a tilted PD from the RSS measured at a few known orientations under one fixed LED: their normalized profile gives the direction to the LED, and their common amplitude gives the distance through a calibrated LED model. A position error bound for an arbitrary receiver response, extended to orientation errors, yields two rules for the tilt of the PD. Maximum-likelihood and ratio-based least-squares estimators are derived for a general response, including the Square model of commercial PDs. Being model-based, they require neither fingerprints nor training: nine measurements calibrate the LED model. On an automated testbed with a ceiling-mounted LED 3~m above the floor, the mean \mbox{3-D} error is 3.66~cm over 125 positions at five heights, and 4.10~cm over 131 random positions estimated live with five orientations. The response model is decisive (77~cm with the cosine response), the remaining error stems from model mismatch, notably the non-Lambertian LED pattern, and 10~ms of signal per orientation suffice.
\end{abstract}
