\section{Estimators for a General Receiver Response}
\label{app:estimators}
This appendix details the estimators of Section~\ref{subsec:estimator} for an arbitrary response $R$, as used with the SQ model, and Algorithm~\ref{alg:estimator} summarizes them. For a cosine-power response, the ratio estimators reduce to the closed form of~\cite{Acuna2026}, recalled at the end.

\emph{Parameterization.} The direction is updated on the sphere through two tangent coordinates $\bm\omega$. With $\vect E$ an orthonormal basis of the plane orthogonal to the current $\vect u$, a step $\Delta\bm\omega$ gives:
\begin{equation}
 {\vect u^+=\cos\kappa\,\vect u+\frac{\sin\kappa}{\kappa}\,\vect E\,\Delta\bm\omega,\qquad\kappa=\norm{\Delta\bm\omega}},
 \label{eq:sphere-step}
\end{equation}
and, since $\partial(\vect n_i\trans\vect u)/\partial\bm\omega\trans=\vect n_i\trans\vect E$, the response changes as $\partial R_i/\partial\bm\omega\trans=\dot R_i\,\vect n_i\trans\vect E$, where $R_i=R(\psi_i)$ and $\dot R_i=\dot R(\psi_i)$, given for SQ in Section~\ref{subsec:fim}.

\emph{NLS.} With $b=\ln\beta$ and $\vect W=\diag(w_1,\ldots,w_K)$, the residual of~\eqref{eq:nls} and its Jacobian with respect to $(\bm\omega,b)$ are:
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
  \vect e&=\vect W^{1/2}\left(\beta\vect R-\vect y\right),\\
  {\vect G}&=\beta\,\vect W^{1/2}\left[\diag(\dot{\vect R})\,\vect N\trans\vect E,\ \vect R\right],
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:nls-jacobian}
\end{equation}
with $\vect R$, $\dot{\vect R}$ and $\vect N$ as in~\eqref{eq:rank}.

\emph{Ratio GLS and WLS.} Expanding the ratios $z_i$ of Section~\ref{subsec:estimator}, with $\bar z_i=R_i/R_j$, to first order in the noise gives $z_i\approx\bar z_i+(\epsilon_i-\bar z_i\epsilon_j)/s_j$, so that their covariance, evaluated at the observations, is up to the common factor $y_j^{-2}$:
\begin{equation}
 {\vect C_z=\diag\!\left(\sigma_i^2\right)_{i\neq j}+\sigma_j^2\,\vect z\vect z\trans},
 \label{eq:ratio-cov}
\end{equation}
where the rank-one term comes from the shared reference. WLS replaces $\vect C_z$ by its diagonal. The rows of the Jacobian of the ratios with respect to $\bm\omega$ are:
\begin{equation}
 {\frac{\partial\bar z_i}{\partial\bm\omega\trans}}=\frac{\dot R_iR_j\,\vect n_i\trans\vect E-R_i\dot R_j\,\vect n_j\trans\vect E}{R_j^2}.
 \label{eq:ratio-jacobian}
\end{equation}
For the SQ response, the ratios $(1-a\psi_i^2)/(1-a\psi_j^2)$ cannot be turned into linear constraints on $\vect u$, unlike the cosine case below, so they are fitted iteratively.

\emph{Solver.} The three fits use the same Levenberg--Marquardt iteration, $(\vect G\trans\vect G+\lambda\vect S)\Delta=-\vect G\trans\vect e$ with damping factor $\lambda$ and $\vect S=\diag(\vect G\trans\vect G)$, where $\vect G$ is the Jacobian of the residual $\vect e$ of the fit, applied through~\eqref{eq:sphere-step}. It is started from several seed directions around the LED axis, none of which uses the true position, and the converged solution with the lowest cost is kept. An estimate is declared invalid if its amplitude is not positive, if an orientation lies on the boundary of the support or if the matrix of~\eqref{eq:rank} is rank-deficient.



\emph{Cosine-power response.} For $R=\cos^{m_R}\psi$, the roots $t_i=z_i^{1/m_R}=\vect n_i\trans\vect u/\vect n_j\trans\vect u$ turn each ratio into a linear constraint, $(\vect n_i-t_i\vect n_j)\trans\vect u=0$. With these constraints stacked as the rows of $\vect B$, GLS and WLS minimize $\vect u\trans\vect B\trans\vect C_t^{-1}\vect B\vect u$ over unit vectors: the solution is the eigenvector of the smallest eigenvalue, with the sign that gives $\vect n_t\trans\vect u<0$. The covariance $\vect C_t$ of the roots follows from~\eqref{eq:ratio-cov} with $y_i$ replaced by $y_i^{1/m_R}$ and $\sigma_i$ by $\sigma_iy_i^{1/m_R-1}/m_R$, and WLS keeps its diagonal. This is the estimator derived in~\cite{Acuna2026} for a reoriented transmitter, with the normals and order of the receiver in place of the steering vectors and Lambertian order of the LED.
